Penobscot Ordinance & State Licensing Reference

Note: This reference file contains verbatim sections from the Town of Penobscot Land Use Regulations, correspondence from the Maine Bureau of Alcoholic Beverages and Lottery Operations (BABLO), and a post-public hearing update on Maine’s Special Amusement and municipal police-power statutes.

Maine BABLO Guidance (Oct. 23, 2026)

Question submitted to BABLO (Oct. 22, 2026):
"Are licenses for on-premises alcohol consumption limited by local population at all? In other words, will the bureau license as many bars or alcohol-serving restaurants as there are qualified applicants, regardless of the population of the town?"

Response from Sara Morey, Licensing and Tax Supervisor, BABLO Liquor Licensing and Enforcement (Oct. 23, 2026):
"The bureau has not established a population-based limit on the number of on-premises liquor licenses that may be issued within a municipality. The Bureau may issue licenses to qualified applicants, provided all applicable state and local licensing requirements are met.

Under 28-A M.R.S. §1051(1)-(2), the Bureau may issue licenses for on-premises consumption to qualified applicants, subject to approval by the municipality in which the establishment is located.

Additionally, 28-A M.R.S. §653(2) outlines the grounds upon which a municipality may deny a liquor license application, including noncompliance with local zoning ordinances, health and safety concerns, and other specified conditions.

While there is no population-based restriction, each application is reviewed individually for compliance with applicable licensing requirements. The municipality must also permit the requested license type under Maine's local option laws."

Post-Public Hearing Update: Standalone Special Amusement & Police-Power Ordinance (28-A M.R.S. § 1054 & 30-A M.R.S. § 3001)

Added following the Penobscot public hearing to reflect points raised by citizens—including a restaurant owner from another state—regarding whether a municipality can enact enforceable noise, hours, and entertainment rules that apply to existing businesses without being blocked by land-use grandfathering.

Under Maine law, a town seeking to regulate noise, hours, and entertainment would generally not adopt those rules solely as an amendment inside its Land Use Regulations. Instead, a practical statutory route is a standalone Special Amusement & Public Nuisance/Noise Ordinance enacted by the legislative body (Town Meeting) under 28-A M.R.S. § 1054 (Special Amusement Permits) and municipal home-rule police power (30-A M.R.S. § 3001).

Why a Standalone Police-Power Ordinance Differs from a Land Use Amendment

1. Vested Rights & Grandfathering Insulation:
• If noise or entertainment controls are embedded solely into land use regulations, existing or pending commercial operations often claim vested rights or nonconforming grandfathered status under statutory zoning protections.
• Special Amusement and noise/nuisance regulations enacted under police power (Title 28-A and Title 30-A) are annual operating licenses and behavioral standards. They apply to every active establishment immediately upon passage—grandfathering does not protect an operator from police-power nuisance or licensing standards.

2. Select Board Administrative Agility:
• Land use amendments require Planning Board public hearings, specific notice windows, and complex site review procedures.
• Under 28-A M.R.S. § 1054, the Penobscot Select Board serves as the municipal licensing authority. The ordinance can empower the Select Board to hold hearings, attach customized conditions (such as closing outdoor seating at 9:00 PM), and suspend or revoke permits quickly if violations occur, without tying up the Planning Board.

3. Avoids the "Zoning" Debate:
• A standalone ordinance is framed around public safety, annual licensing, and neighbor protection, avoiding broader debates over property rights or commercial land classification.

Architecture of an Interim "Special Amusement & Operational Noise" Ordinance for Penobscot
Neighboring coastal towns—most notably Brooksville (which also lacks traditional zoning) and Sedgwick—rely on this model. Penobscot can adapt their proven mechanisms into a compact ordinance built around four key pillars:

1. Mandatory Special Amusement Permit (28-A M.R.S. § 1054):
• Trigger: Any on-premises liquor licensee in Penobscot offering entertainment, live vocal or instrumental music, DJs, or amplified sound (excluding incidental background radio or television at conversational levels) must obtain an annual permit from the Penobscot Select Board.
• Hearing & Abutter Notice: Mandate that upon application, written notice is sent via first-class mail to all property owners within 300–500 feet, followed by a public hearing prior to permit issuance or annual renewal.

2. Fixed Curfews for Amplified & Outdoor Activity:
• Sample Standard (modeled on Sedgwick & Blue Hill practices): Outdoor music, entertainment, and amplified speakers must cease promptly at 9:00 PM; indoor entertainment and music must cease at 10:30 PM or 11:00 PM; exterior patio closing hours can be conditioned independently of kitchen hours.

3. Objective Noise & Emitted Sound Standards:
• Plainly Audible Rule: "No sound from musical instruments, amplifiers, loudspeakers, or patron gatherings shall be plainly audible within an abutting residential dwelling or beyond 100 feet of the property line between 9:00 PM and 7:00 AM."
• Decibel Benchmark (Optional): Setting property-line limits (e.g., 60 dBA daytime / 50 dBA night, mirroring Castine and Maine DEP Chapter 375 guidelines).

4. Discretionary Conditioning Authority:
• Explicitly authorize the Penobscot Select Board to attach binding written conditions to any permit based on site characteristics, including required orientation of outdoor seating or speakers away from residential abutters, prohibition of outdoor amplified sound on constrained parcels, required parking management plans (preventing overflow parking on public roads), and revocation/suspension triggers if two or more validated noise or disorderly complaints occur within a license year.

Bridging to a Future Commercial Site Plan Review
• Cross-Reference Clause: State that any Special Amusement Permit is subject to all other municipal ordinances, and that compliance with future site plan standards will become a condition of permit renewal once adopted.
• Immediate Implementation: A Special Amusement Ordinance can be prepared and voted on at a Special Town Meeting independently, giving Penobscot an immediate regulatory framework over noise, late hours, and outdoor entertainment as soon as any alcohol license is activated. (As with all statutory questions on this site, voters and officials are encouraged to review any proposed ordinance language with the town’s attorney or the Maine Municipal Association.)

Land Use Regulation - Section 6.L: Soils

L. Soils.
1. All land uses shall be located on soils in or upon which the proposed uses or structures can be established or maintained without causing adverse environmental impacts, including severe erosion, mass soil movement, and water pollution, whether during or after construction. Proposed used requiring subsurface waste disposal, and commercial or industrial development and other similar intensive land used, shall require a soils report, prepared by a State-certified soil scientist or geologist based on an on-site investigation. Suitability considerations shall be based primarily on criteria employed in the National Cooperative Soil Survey as modified by on-site factors such as depth to water table and depth to refusal.

Land Use Regulation - Section 12.B: Permits (Subsections 6 & 7)

6. Permits Issued by Planning Board
The Planning Board shall approve or deny those applications on which it is empowered to act as stated in this Ordinance. The Planning Board may, after the submission of a complete application including all information requested, grant a permit if it makes a positive finding based on the information presented to it that, except as specifically exempted in this Ordinance, the proposed use:
a. Will not result in unsafe or unhealthful conditions;
b. Will not result in erosion or sedimentation;
c. Will not result in water pollution;
d. Will not result in damage to spawning grounds, fish, aquatic life, bird and other wildlife habitat;
e. Will conserve shoreland vegetation;
f. Will conserve visual points of access to waters as viewed from public facilities;
g. Will conserve actual points of public access to waters;
h. Will conserve natural beauty;
i. Will avoid problems associated with flood plan development and use;
j. Is in conformance with the provisions of Section 6, Land Use Standard

7. Conditions
Permits granted under this Section may be made subject to reasonable conditions to ensure conformity with the purposes and provisions of this Ordinance.

Land Use Regulation - Section 7: Structures (7.5, 7.10 & 7.11)

7.5 There shall be a 20 foot setback on the side lot lines and rear lot lines of all structures in any District.

7.10 There shall be no commercial establishments within the bounds of the Shore-land District or within the bounds of sub-divisions for residential purposes.

7.11 Professional offices such as doctors, lawyers, insurance agents, dentists, optometrists, beauty parlors are the only type allowed in sub-division for residential purposes or the Shoreland District.
